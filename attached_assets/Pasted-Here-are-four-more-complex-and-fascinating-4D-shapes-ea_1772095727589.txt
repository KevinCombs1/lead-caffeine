Here are four more complex and fascinating 4D shapes, each with a unique structure and a complete, self-contained code file for you to explore.

4D Orthoplex (16-Cell): The 4D analogue of an octahedron. It consists of 8 tetrahedral cells, 24 triangular faces, 32 edges, and 8 vertices. It can be visualized as two interlocking tetrahedral "pyramids" in 4D space. Its projection is a beautiful, symmetrical structure of intersecting lines.

4D Duoprism (Torus Duoprism): A 4D shape formed by the Cartesian product of two 2D shapes. A common example is the Torus Duoprism, which is the product of two circles. It's topologically equivalent to a 4D hypertorus but is constructed differently, resulting in a "grid" of interlocking rings.

4D Hypercone: The 4D analogue of a cone. It has a 3D spherical base and a single apex point in the fourth dimension. As you rotate it in the W-axis, the sphere shrinks to a point and then grows back on the "other side," creating a stunning visual of a 4D extrusion.

4D Great Rhombicuboctahedron (Truncated Cuboctahedron): This is one of the Archimedean solids, extended into 4D. It's a highly complex shape with 26 cells: 8 truncated cubes, 18 octagonal prisms, and 12 cubes. Its projection is an incredibly dense and intricate web of polygons.
